# 06 — Data Protection Authority, Enforcement, Penalties & Exemptions

## The Data Protection Authority (DPA) — Part V (in force since July 2023)
- Body corporate established by s.28; Board of 5–7 members appointed by the President (s.29); Chairperson appointed by the President (s.30); Director-General is CEO (s.36, Part VI).
- **Objects (s.31):** regulate processing; safeguard privacy from adverse effects of digitalisation; protect data in digital transactions; ensure compliance to support digital-economy growth.
- **Key powers (s.32):** inspect information held by controllers/processors; direct compliance (incl. publishing processing terms); direct **reimbursement of fees** to data subjects; conduct inquiries, receive complaints, summon persons, issue directives and **impose fines**; examine under oath; **enter premises, inspect or seize records** where processing poses an imminent risk; periodic evaluations incl. of the DPMP; appoint advisory committees; recognise certification bodies; set standards; make rules, guidelines and directives.
- **Duties (s.33)** include: direct compliance with ss.11 and 13; monitor processing on its own motion or at a data subject's request; **issue directives to specific controllers/processors (s.33(c))** — also the route for cross-border instruments under new s.26(2); training; DPMP directives; promote self-regulation; advise government; research new technologies; rules on data sharing between public authorities via a secure interoperability platform; advisory committees for **sectoral guidelines**; rules on special categories, marketing messages, profiling and automated decisions.
- **Licences (s.34):** for identity-management and related services, on advisory-committee recommendations; categories and procedure to be prescribed.
- **Guidelines (s.51A, new 2025):** DPA may issue guidelines on any matter in the Act, incl. sectoral guidelines and the DPMP.
- **Rules (s.52):** first rules due within **36 months** of Part V commencement (2025 amendment; was 24). Rules on rights, refusal criteria and other matters need **≥2 weeks public consultation**, Minister's approval, Gazette publication and Parliament approval within 3 months.
- **Regulations (s.53):** by the Minister with DPA concurrence (e.g. amending Schedules I–IV, fees, licences, safeguards, appeal procedure). The power to list "adequate" third countries (old s.53(2)(b)) was **repealed** in 2025.

## Inquiries & directives (s.35 — Part V, in force)
If, on a complaint or otherwise, the DPA believes a controller/processor is contravening, or about to contravene, the Act (or rules/regulations/guidelines/Orders under it, or other written law on personal data processing), it may hold an **inquiry** (procedure to be prescribed; draft Oct 2024). After a hearing, it may issue a **written, binding directive** to:
(a) **cease and refrain** from the conduct;
(b) **perform acts** needed to rectify the situation;
(c) **pay compensation** determined by the DPA to an aggrieved person who suffered harm, loss or damage.
Directives are sent by registered post or electronically and are binding from communication.

## Penalties — Part VII (s.38–39) — **no operational date yet**
- Failure to comply with a **s.35 directive** → penalty **up to LKR 10,000,000 (ten million rupees) for each non-compliance** (s.38(1)).
- **Repeat offenders:** if previously penalised and fail again, an **additional penalty of twice the amount** imposed, for the second and each subsequent non-compliance (s.38(2)).
- Penalties go to the Consolidated Fund after deducting compensation to aggrieved persons (s.38(3)).
- Unpaid penalties recoverable through the **Magistrate's Court of Colombo** (ex parte), even above its normal fine limits (s.38(4)).
- Other regulators can still suspend/cancel licences (s.38(5)).
- **Personal liability (s.38(6)):** for a company, every **director and officer responsible for management and control** at the time; for a firm, every **partner** — unless they prove **no knowledge** or **all due care and diligence**.
- **Appeal** to the Court of Appeal within **21 working days** of the penalty notice; must **deposit cash equal to the penalty**; **burden of proof on the controller/processor** (s.38(7)–(10)).

**Factors for setting a penalty (s.39):** nature, gravity and duration; number of people affected and damage; mitigation steps; **effectiveness of the DPMP**; cooperation with the DPA; categories of data; whether the organisation **self-reported**; previous non-compliance; other aggravating/mitigating factors such as financial gain or losses avoided.

Plain words: **The fine is for ignoring the DPA's order, not automatically for every mistake.** But a mistake can lead to an order, and directors can be personally liable. Self-reporting, cooperating and having a real DPMP reduce the risk.

## Exemptions (s.40 — sits in Part VII, no operational date yet)
No exemption, restriction or derogation is allowed **unless provided for by law**, respecting the essence of fundamental rights, and **necessary and proportionate in a democratic society** for:
(a) national security, defence, public safety, public health, economic and financial system stability;
(b) judicial impartiality and independence;
(c) prevention, investigation and prosecution of crime;
(d) execution of criminal penalties;
(e) protection of rights and freedoms of persons, **particularly freedom of expression and the right to information**.

Also relevant: refusal grounds for rights requests (s.17(2)), Schedule IV (criminal investigations), household exemption (s.2(3)).

## Other points
- DPA officers: protected for acts in good faith (s.49); deemed **public servants** under the Penal Code (s.50); DPA is a scheduled institution under the **Bribery Act** (s.51); strict secrecy declaration (s.54).
