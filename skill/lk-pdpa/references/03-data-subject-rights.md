# 03 — Data Subject Rights (Part II, as amended 2025)

**Status:** Part II (s.13–19) — **no operational date appointed yet** (as of 23 Oct 2026). Explain the rights, but say clearly that the formal right to make these requests and appeal to the DPA starts only when the Minister gazettes Part II. Organisations should prepare processes now (s.12(1)(h), operational 1 Jan 2027, already requires the DPMP to facilitate these rights).

All requests are **in writing** ("written" includes electronic — email/web form is fine).

## The rights

| Right | Section | What it means | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Access** | s.13 | Confirm whether your data is processed, and get the Schedule V information | Subject to s.17 refusal grounds |
| **Withdraw consent** | s.14(1) | Withdraw at any time where processing is based on consent (Sch I(a) / Sch II(a)) | Earlier processing stays lawful |
| **Object / ask to stop** ("refrain from further processing") | s.14(2) | Where processing is based on public task (Sch I(e)), legitimate interests (Sch I(f)) or Sch II(f) | |
| **Rectification / completion** | s.15 | Correct inaccurate data or complete incomplete data, without undue delay | No duty to collect extra unnecessary data; if data must be kept as evidence under law/court order, controller must stop processing it instead of changing it |
| **Erasure** | s.16 | Delete data where: (a) processed in breach of ss.5–11; (b) consent withdrawn; (c) erasure required by law or court order | Only these three grounds |
| **Review of automated decisions** | s.18 | Ask for review of a decision based **solely** on automated processing that has created or is likely to create an **irreversible and continuous impact** on rights and freedoms **under the Constitution or any written law** (2025 wording) | Not applicable where authorised by law, authorised as determined by the DPA, based on consent, or necessary for a contract (but the contract exception does **not** apply to special categories). Controller must follow DPA safeguard rules |
| **Appeal to the DPA** | s.19 | Appeal a controller's refusal | See below |

## Who can make the request (s.17(5))
- Parent / person with parental authority / legal guardian for a **minor**
- Court-appointed guardian/administrator for a person physically or mentally unfit
- A person **authorised in writing** by the data subject
- An **heir**, for a deceased person, **within 10 years** of death
(manner to be prescribed by regulations)

## Controller's response (s.17 as amended in 2025)

- Inform the requester **in writing, without undue delay and within ONE MONTH** of receipt whether the request was **granted** or **refused with reasons** (unless a law prohibits disclosing reasons). *(Original 2022 text said 21 working days — replaced.)*
- **Extension:** for reasons given, a further **two months**, total **not exceeding three months** from receipt. The requester must be told about the extension **before the first month ends**.
- Must inform the requester of the **right to appeal to the DPA** if refused.
- **Free of charge.** The DPA may make rules on criteria/circumstances when a fee may be charged. *(Original fee-by-regulation provisions removed.)*
- If identity is unclear, the controller may ask for **additional information** (s.17(4)).

### Grounds to refuse (s.17(2)) — applies to requests under ss.13, 14, 15, 16 and 18
(a) national security; (b) public order; (c) an inquiry/investigation/procedure under written law; (d) prevention, detection, investigation or prosecution of crime; (e) rights and freedoms of others under written law; (f) technical and operational feasibility (after trying to verify identity); (g) cannot establish identity (after asking for more info); (h) processing required under written law.
The controller must **record reasons** for refusals and give them to the DPA on written request (s.17(3)).

## Appeal to the DPA (s.19 as amended)
A data subject may appeal where the controller refused:
(a) access (s.13); (b) withdrawal of consent (s.14(1)) or stopping processing (s.14(2)); (c) rectification/completion (s.15); (d) erasure (s.16); (e) a request under s.17(2); (f) review of an automated decision (s.18(1)).
- Form, manner and time limit: **to be prescribed** (draft regulations Oct 2024 exist — draft only).
- The DPA investigates and decides, within a prescribed period, whether to **allow or disallow** (with reasons), and informs both parties.
- If allowed, the controller must give effect within the period set by the DPA and report back (s.19(4)).
- Either party may appeal the DPA decision to the **Court of Appeal within 30 days** (s.19(5)).

## Plain-language explainer (use for public)

- **"Can I ask a company what data they have about me?"** Yes — the right of access. Write to them (email is fine). They must answer within one month (can extend to 3 months total if they explain). It should be free.
- **"Can I make them delete my data?"** Only if they used it unlawfully, you withdrew consent they relied on, or a law/court requires deletion.
- **"I keep getting promo SMS."** That is mainly Part IV (marketing messages) — see `05-direct-marketing.md`. You can also withdraw consent under s.14.
- **"A bank's system rejected my loan automatically."** You may ask for a human review if the decision was solely automated and has a serious, lasting impact — subject to exceptions (e.g. contract, consent).
