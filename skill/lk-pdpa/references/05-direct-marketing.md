# 05 — Marketing Messages ("Solicited Messages") — Part IV, s.27

**Status:** Part IV — **no operational date appointed yet** (as of 23 Oct 2026). The original 24–48-month window was removed by the 2025 amendment; the Minister will appoint the date by Gazette.

## The rule
A controller may use **post, telecom, electronic or similar means** to send promotional messages **only if the person has consented** to receive them ("solicited messages"), subject to s.14 (withdrawal of consent).

- Consent must meet **Schedule III** (provable, clearly separate, plain language, not a condition of an unrelated service, told they can withdraw).
- **At the time of collecting contact details AND in every message**, tell the person how to **opt out free of charge**.
- Each message must state the **nature of the message** and the **identity of the controller** (or third party on whose behalf it is sent).
- The DPA may, with the relevant regulator (e.g. TRCSL), set **codes or prefixes** to identify categories of marketing messages.

## What is a "message"?
Any written, electronic, oral, pictorial or video message intended to promote:
(a) goods or services of the controller or a third party; or
(b) any person, entity or organisation, including the controller —
sent by post, electronic or telecom services or similar, **including automated calling/communication systems with or without human involvement**.

**Excluded:** internet-based advertisements to which the person consented in order to obtain a **free** service from the controller (e.g. ads shown inside a free app the user signed up for).

## Examples (explain simply)
| Situation | OK? |
|---|---|
| Promo SMS to customers who ticked "Send me offers" with an opt-out ("Reply STOP free") | ✅ |
| Buying a phone-number list and sending bulk SMS | ❌ no consent |
| Robocall promoting a political candidate to random numbers | ❌ "promote any person" + automated calling, no consent |
| WhatsApp broadcast to customers who never agreed | ❌ |
| Transactional SMS (OTP, delivery update, bill reminder) | Not promotional — not Part IV, but Part I still applies |
| Ads inside a free mobile game the user installed | Excluded from Part IV |

## Practical checklist (prepare now)
- ✅ Unticked, separate "Yes, send me offers" checkbox
- ✅ Record when/how each consent was obtained (Schedule III(a))
- ✅ Free opt-out in every message (e.g. "Reply STOP to 77xx — free")
- ✅ Sender name clearly identifies your business
- ✅ Honour opt-outs fast; keep a suppression list
- ✅ Check vendor SMS gateways are bound as processors (s.21)
